> **A note:** I am not a philosopher, and I am not claiming that these thoughts
> are correct, or even that they make sense. I simply had fun thinking and
> reflecting. The possibility that someone might read what I was thinking at
> this point in time gives me a little joy.

These are fragments rather than one continuous argument. They circle the same
uncertain territory: what remains ours, what makes something valuable, and what
every choice takes away.

## When AI can make art

If AI can create art and cause an emotional reaction, what is left for human
creativity?

Perhaps humans will attach a higher value to work made by a human mind than to
work made by a machine. But will we always think like that? And if not, what is
left for us?

Anything expressed in forms, shapes, or abstract art becomes an embodied human
creation, something that can be objectified. If AI can occupy all of those forms,
perhaps what remains is internal human creation: our thoughts and the direct
communication between us. Maybe the most human art is not the object that can
be stored, copied, and displayed, but the subjective and unembodied interaction
that is only perceived.

> Believing in the value of something creates value.

## Restlessness

Resting and not moving can feel like a lack of progress, even a resignation. It
can feel like accepting the average in life and giving up an inner ambition for
the lazy current state of things.

In that sense, I allow my innermost self to give itself up, because that self is
forward-looking and longs for a better future. I do not know what that future
means. Maybe it is simply a change in any direction.

The irony is that whatever I was searching for may already have been there. I
could not see it because I could see only one color: no contrast, no shapes, no
reference, nothing to understand or compare. How can anything, even a feeling, be
valuable if I do not know its contrast or the alternative path?

## A value without comparison

Is true love the only real thing that has value as a self-embodied truth? Is
love a value that cannot be compared or transferred?

I do not even know exactly what love is. I do not think I believe in it. It may
be just a concept.

Restlessness.

> Home is wherever I am not. Longing but not belonging. Even moving feels like
> rest when there is no change in direction or speed. Travelling started a
> journey and forgot the destination.

## The choice after the choice

Suppose “freedom of choice” exists, without touching the larger question of free
will.

I can choose to work through the night because I want to. The next morning,
tired and exhausted, do I really have a choice about drinking a strong coffee?

On some days I may still refuse it. My careful inner will, the part I imagine as
independent of real-world constraints, might be stronger. On most days, I will
drink the coffee.

Or take an even more obvious example: I am in Spain, where I cannot get proper
sourdough bread. I chose to be here, but I did not choose the bread available
here.

The irony is that a true moment of free will, if it exists, restricts future
freedom of choice. A decision creates the conditions under which the next
decision must be made. Freedom of choice is therefore momentary; it cannot exist
unchanged beyond the moment, because every choice constrains what follows.
